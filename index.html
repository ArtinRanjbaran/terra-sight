A SHORT FIELD GUIDE

How to read this observatory.

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) sends microwave pulses and measures their echoes. It can observe at night and through clouds; repeat passes let researchers compare how a surface scatters radar energy.

  1. Choose a place and dates. Query NASA CMR for real OPERA RTC-S1 acquisitions.
  2. Inspect the evidence. Browse previews and map footprints help you assess coverage, not movement.
  3. Compare compatible rasters. Authenticated products and matching quality masks are required for derived backscatter change.

Backscatter change is not a displacement measurement. This prototype does not implement InSAR, coherence, or glacier velocity.
